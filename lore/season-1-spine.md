# THE FIRST WAR — THE SEASON ONE SPINE

*The arc document. Two beats are fixed by what really happened. Everything after is pressure, not prophecy.*

---

## I. THE QUESTION

The war is not over land. There is no city to sack, no river to hold. Roman Slate sits a throne no one has taken in living memory, and the Vanguard exists for one proposition: **that the reign can end.** The Dominion does not need to win. It needs only for nothing to change. That asymmetry is the engine of the whole season.

So the question Season One asks is narrow and brutal: **can the outcome be moved at all?**

Two weeks in, the honest answer is *not yet* — and that is the story.

The other eight fights of those two weeks were an order fighting itself — trials and court politics, real for a legend's standing, worth nothing to the war. **Six of the fourteen legends have not yet faced an enemy at all.** Cassia Dawn has never stood across from the throne she was founded to break. Brother Amos has never met the false saint who counterfeits him. Two weeks of maximum violence and the two rivalries the whole myth is built on have not happened.

---

## III. WHAT THE DEADLOCK MEANS

Here is the cruelty in the arithmetic. Across both weeks the Vanguard has scored **1,710.53** to the Dominion's **1,656.70**. In the cross-faction fights alone — the ones that count — the Vanguard has put up **763.77** against **635.42**, a surplus of **128.35**, and holds **exactly nothing**. The heroes are winning on weight and losing on placement. Every point spent in a rout against a beaten enemy is a point that bought no ground.

That is the Dominion's entire thesis rendered as a number. Roman Slate does not have to out-fight anyone. He has to make effort not matter.

And it cuts the other way too. The Dominion came into this expecting a short correction and got a wall. It has not advanced either. Both orders spent two weeks at full violence and woke on the same line they started on.

There is a second silence worth naming. **No one has yet won a close fight that counted.** Close fights have happened — Juno Reyes took Titus Vale by **7.32**, Vesper Kane took Dr. Orin Pyre by **14.41** — but both were fought inside an order, where nothing is at stake but standing. Across the line, where the war is decided, the narrowest anyone has managed is Mave's **14.33**, and four of the six were routs. **Nerve has been tested at home and never at the front.** Season One's first genuinely close cross-faction result will be the loudest event of the year, precisely because two weeks of scoreboards have refused to produce one.

## IV. THE THREADS THE REAL WEEKS HANDED US

### The Undrafted and the Dynasty
Rook Callahan opened the war with **172.86** — the second-highest mark anyone has posted — and dismantled the Rust King by 84.49. For one week the man nobody picked was the most dangerous thing on the field. Then the throne answered — **170.18 – 105.28**. Slate had been routed the week before and came into this one with nothing banked; he did not out-compound Rook Callahan, he simply put up the biggest week of his life at the moment refusal had nothing left to refuse.

This is the season's cleanest arc and it belongs to the commissioner's own character. *The Undrafted proved them all wrong, and the establishment made an example of him for it.* The pressure now: Rook's first earned title, *"…Unbenched,"* is written and unpaid, and he is seven points of Legend short of it. Whether he climbs back is the Vanguard's proof-of-concept for the entire rebellion.

### The Oathkeeper, undefeated
**Eli Thorne is 2–0 across the line and won both as routs** — 167.50 – 87.97 over the throne itself, then 132.16 – 69.31 over the Gilded. He leads every legend in the war, claimed or free. He is one of three legends still unbeaten — Brother Amos and Countess Mave are also 2–0 — but he is the only one with two cross-faction wins and no cross-faction loss: the only legend who has taken the field twice against an enemy and never once been answered.

And he has no rival. The canon gives him no opposite number, no nemesis, no +3 waiting anywhere on the board. **The strongest legend in The First War is fighting a war that has not yet named an enemy for him.** Season One's job is to find him one — or to let the Dominion do it.

### The Gilded rose, then broke
Saint Vega won on the lowest winning mark either week produced — **88.97 – 69.68**. A week later Eli Thorne left him at **69.31** — the lowest score anyone has posted in the history of the war. The false shepherd took his one prize by scraping, then was emptied out in public.

The thread this arms is the one the canon already pre-wrote and has never been able to pay: Brother Amos holds the title *"The Shepherd Who Broke the Gilded"* in escrow, and the two of them have never met. **Amos is 2–0 and has beaten only his own** — including the Vanguard's founder, 126.78 – 102.16. He is undefeated against his family and untested against his enemy.

### The Vampire fed on her own
Countess Mave posted **176.71** in Week 1 — the highest score of the entire war — and it came against Vesper Kane, a sister of her own order. **The Vampire's greatest feast was on the Dominion's own blood.** It bought the throne nothing.

Then Week 2 put the thirst across from endurance: **130.62 – 116.29 over Marcus Kell**, the closest cross-faction margin yet, 14.33 — siphon against the man built to still be standing after the biggest hit anyone can throw. Kell lost — and grew, three feats out of a defeat. *He is the proof the Dominion fears most: being knocked down is not the same as being finished.* That rematch is the Dominion's most interesting unfinished business, because both powers feed on a long fight: she grows by taking what is in front of her, he grows by being hit and banking it — every blow he endures leaves him harder to move, never weaker. The question is which one runs out of the other first.

### And one nobody planned: both Vale brothers are on the floor
Titus Vale and Silas Vane are each **0–2**, each sitting at zero Legend, and in Week 2 **each was put down by his own side** — Titus by Juno Reyes, Silas by Ezra Cain. The iron and the rust — the war's tightest blood-thread — and the war has not once let them reach each other. Each has spent two weeks being spent on someone else's quarrel. That is not a subplot the writers get to skip: it is a brother on each side of a line neither chose, both paying for it, and neither yet given the one fight that would mean anything to him. When the front finally puts rust across from iron, it will be the oldest grievance in the story and the only one with blood in it.

## V. WHERE THE GREAT DAYS FALL

The weekly war is unscheduled. The campaign's three great days are appointed.

- **THE FIRST CLASH — Week 5.** Top four against top four, chosen on accumulated Legend alone. Three weeks out. Among the claimed fourteen, today the Vanguard's four would be Eli Thorne, Rook Callahan, Brother Amos and Cassia Dawn; the Dominion's would be Countess Mave, Ezra Cain, and the throne beside the thief. **Marcus Kell, Juno Reyes and Titus Vale are all outside the cut, and so is Silas Vane.** For any of them to march, someone has to have a week big enough to displace a name above them — and the merit race, not the schedule, is what decides who stands on the field. One thing the bible never spells out: selection is stated as pure merit, and the six Free Legends all sit at 8 on the veteran floor — above Cassia Dawn, Roman Slate, Vesper Kane and Ezra Cain, and level with Rook Callahan and Brother Amos. Whether they are merit-eligible before the Last Front is unruled, and equal Legend at the cutline has no tie-break. The Clash ends in a retreat or an interruption. Nobody dies. Nothing is settled.
- **THE SIEGE — Week 9.** Six against six. Wider, so the middle of each order gets its day.
- **THE LAST FRONT — Week 17.** All ten against all ten, including the six who never had a manager. The only decisive day of the year, and the one that crowns the war.

Two things govern whether any of this has teeth. **First: someone has to break the deadlock.** The High Ground bonus carried into the Last Front is set by the front's position, and a front at dead even carries **nothing at all** — the entire season's war currently buys neither side a single point of advantage in the finale. **Second: no Ultimate is coming to save anyone.** No Ultimate has ever fired, and none can. The threshold is 24 Legend and the leader of the whole board stands at 11 — but even at 24 there is no Unleash to press; the engine has never passed an ultimate into a battle. Twenty comeback moves are loaded into a weapon with no trigger, and until that changes every legend fights with his hands only.

---

## VI. WHAT WINNING LOOKS LIKE

**For the Dominion, victory is the calendar.** Roman Slate wins by Week 17 arriving with the line still where it started. He does not need the front; he needs it to stay boring. The Dynasty only compounds what it takes, so a frozen line banks him nothing — it simply spends the one thing the Vanguard cannot get back, and his own warning runs the other way: *far easier to beat early than late.*

**For the Vanguard, victory is a single inch, proven.** Not the finale — the demonstration. The heroes have already shown they can out-score the Dominion and it changed nothing, which means the thing they have to learn is *where* to spend it. One cross-faction week taken clean would do what two weeks of routs could not: move the map, and prove to a thinner order that the reign is not a law of nature.

And beneath the myth there is only one house. The founder has not yet faced the throne. The shepherd has not yet faced the saint. Two brothers are on the floor on opposite sides of a line neither can reach across. **The war is currently a tie, and a tie is not nothing — it is the Dominion winning slowly.**

The ending is not written. The season itself gets a vote.
