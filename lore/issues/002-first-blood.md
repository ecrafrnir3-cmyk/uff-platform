# THE FIRST WAR
## ISSUE #002 — "FIRST BLOOD"
### Week One of the Inaugural War · Seven fronts, one Sunday

---

**PAGE ONE — SPLASH**

**Panel 1 — Before the Line**

No city. No river. No named country. The war has never bothered with geography — it has only ever had *the line*, and the line is wherever two of the twenty are standing when the week begins.

Fourteen champions take the field for the first time. Seven fronts open at once. The Dominion's throne sits behind its own ranks with Roman Slate on it, unbothered, a man who has won so often that the winning has gone quiet in him. Across the gap, Cassia Dawn counts her torches. There are fewer than there used to be.

Nobody knows yet what this war costs. By nightfall, three of them will.

> **CAPTION:** *They called it the First War because they assumed there would be a second. Nobody had thought past Sunday.*

---

**PAGE TWO–FOUR — THE FIRST FRONT**

**Panel 2 — What Rust Expects**

Silas Vane came to the line the way rot comes to a beam: patiently, certain of the arithmetic. He had beaten better men than the one in front of him by simply standing near them long enough. *Iron is what rust was made to eat.* He had built a whole life on that sentence.

But the man across from him was not iron.

**Panel 3 — Nobody's Pick**

Rook Callahan had no gift. That is the whole of him and the whole of his danger. Corrosion needs something to eat, and refusal is not a metal.

He hit first and he did not stop hitting. **Explosion** — one strike that went through the Rust King's guard and out the other side. Then **Breakaway**, ground eaten in a single run, more field crossed in one breath than Silas had planned to concede all day. Then **Stonewall**, the counter-blow caught and held. Then, because the day had decided to be ridiculous, **Twist of Fate** — the Rust King's own momentum turned in his hands like a wet rope.

Four feats. One man. Only one other champion on the board managed four — and she was wearing the other colors.

**Panel 4 — 172.86 to 88.37**

Silas Vane earned exactly one thing all afternoon: a **Stonewall**, a single moment where he held. It is in the record. It is the only thing in the record with his name on it.

He walked off the front under his own power, corroding nothing, having found the one opponent whose strength is the absence of anything to corrode.

> *Won the game 172.86–88.37 · Won the Battle. The margin was eighty-four and a half points, and the Vanguard's front moved.*

> **SILAS (low, to no one):** "Iron rusts. He isn't iron."

---

**PAGE FIVE–SEVEN — THE THRONE, TESTED**

**Panel 5 — The Dynasty on the Field**

Roman Slate does not warm up. He arrives already having won, in his own estimation, and waits for the field to agree with him. Every advantage he takes compounds into the next. Beat him early or do not beat him at all.

Eli Thorne beat him early.

**Panel 6 — The Spoken Oath**

Eli's power only works if he says it out loud, which is a strange and reckless way to be strong. He said it out loud.

Then he made it true three times over. **Explosion** — the vow to strike, kept. **Breakaway** — the vow to reach the mark, kept, the distance closed before the Dominion could organize a second thought. **Twist of Fate** — the vow to hold, kept, and the omen that came with it.

Three feats, and the throne never got to compound anything. The snowball needs a slope. Eli took the slope away in the first exchange and never gave it back.

**Panel 7 — 167.50 to 87.97**

The Dynasty finished the week with nothing in the ledger. No feat. No moment. Seventy-nine and a half points of daylight between the establishment and a young man who told everyone exactly what he was going to do before he did it.

> *Won the game 167.50–87.97 · Won the Battle. The Vanguard's front moved.*

> **ROMAN (private, to the empty seat beside the throne):** "Good. Do it again."

> *He meant it. That is the part nobody will believe.*

---

**PAGE EIGHT–NINE — THE SNAKE EATS ITSELF (I)**

**Panel 8 — Court Business**

Two Dominion banners met, and the war meter did not so much as twitch. This was not warfare. This was the court, settling something, and the court settles things with a smile and a knife.

Dr. Orin Pyre against Ezra Cain. The Negation against the Fallen Warden. One man who unmakes gifts, one man who seals possessions shut so no hand can reach in — and both of them standing under the same flag, which meant the knives had to come out sideways.

Pyre found the flaw. He always does; it is the only thing that makes him feel alive. **Stonewall**, **Breakaway**, **Twist of Fate** — three of them, stacked, while Ezra held what he could hold: **Stonewall**, **Twist of Fate**, two marks in the ledger and not enough of them.

**127.15 to 107.32.** Nineteen points and change. Close enough that the court noticed, wide enough that nobody could call it luck.

> *Won the game 127.15–107.32 · Won the Duel. The front did not move. This was politics.*

> **PYRE:** "You held. You always hold. It's the leaving you're good at."

Ezra said nothing. Ezra is very good at saying nothing about the things that cost him most.

---

**PAGE TEN — THE ONLY WOUND**

**Panel 9 — The Gilded Day**

Saint Vega won the lowest-scoring front of the week and he will tell the story for a year.

Juno Reyes came in the way she always comes in — first, already there, faster than the doubt that catches other people. But her edge is a fragile thing: steal one tempo back and the sharpening resets to nothing. Vega took the moment off her. **Stonewall** — the Gilded closing his hand over a moment he had no right to, and the run she had been building died where it stood. Then **Twist of Fate**, and the day turned gilded.

**88.97 to 69.68.** Nineteen points. No feats for the Skybreaker at all — the only Vanguard champion to take the field and leave it with an empty ledger.

> *Lost the game 69.68–88.97 · Lost the Battle. The Dominion took ground.*

**Panel 10 — What It Cost**

It was the only front the Vanguard lost to the Dominion, and it was the quietest fight on the board. Two teams under a hundred points. No spectacle, no highlight, nothing for the statue.

Vega is going to build a monument to it anyway. That is what he is for.

> **VEGA:** "Glory shared is glory wasted."

Juno did not answer. She was already running.

---

**PAGE ELEVEN — THE SNAKE EATS ITSELF (II)**

**Panel 11 — The Vampire and the Thief**

Countess Mave put up **176.71** — the highest number any champion, of either order, reached in the opening week of the First War. She did it against Vesper Kane, who put up **153.37**, which would have won three of the other six fronts on the board.

Two Dominion women, drinking from the same well, and one of them drank faster.

Mave took **Explosion**, **Stonewall**, **Twist of Fate**. Kane took **Explosion**, **Stonewall**, **Breakaway**, **Twist of Fate** — four feats, the same count as Rook Callahan, the best individual haul on the villain side — and still lost by **23.34**.

**Panel 12 — The Arithmetic of Parasites**

That is the Dominion in one panel: the second-best week anyone in the Dominion had all Sunday (fifth-best in the league), thrown away against her own banner, moving the war not one inch. Kane earned everything and gained nothing but Legend. Mave earned less and won the field.

> *Won the game 176.71–153.37 · Won the Duel. The front did not move. Nothing was decided. Everything was noted.*

> **MAVE:** "You built that. I'll keep it."

---

**PAGE TWELVE — TRIALS OF THE VANGUARD**

**Panel 13 — First Light Against the Wall**

Cassia Dawn and Titus Vale, two heroes, no war at stake — only standing, and standing matters to people who have nothing else to hold.

The founder took it **162.68 to 120.09**. **Explosion** and **Twist of Fate** for the First Light; a lone **Twist of Fate** for the Sentinel, who set his feet and hardened and held and was simply outshone. Ironset only pays while he refuses to give ground. He gave none. It was not enough, and there is no shame in it, and Titus will not believe that for a while.

**Panel 14 — The Shepherd Takes the Reckoning**

Brother Amos against Marcus Kell. **124.59 to 97.14.**

Amos brought **Explosion** and **Twist of Fate**. Marcus brought **Stonewall** and **Twist of Fate**, soaked what came, grew steadier as it dragged, and lost anyway — because the man across from him wasn't trying to break him. Amos doesn't break people. He gathers them. Marcus walked off harder to move than he'd walked on, which is the only victory his power has ever promised him.

> *Two Trials of the Vanguard. Two heroes beaten by two heroes. The front did not move an inch.*

---

**PAGE THIRTEEN — CLOSING**

**Panel 15 — The Count**

Seven fronts. Only three of them were war.

Rook Callahan drove Silas Vane from the field. Eli Thorne drove Roman Slate from the field. Saint Vega drove Juno Reyes from the field.

**Vanguard two. Dominion one.** The line moved — barely, the width of one contested Sunday — and every champion who bled for it now knows the shape of the thing they signed up for.

**Panel 16 — What the Week Actually Proved**

Twenty-nine feats were earned across the league. Not one of them was **Ice Water**. Not one. Nobody won by five points or fewer; nobody stood in a closing moment with the whole thing balanced on their nerve and found out what they were made of.

Between them the two orders have filled every attribute but that one. The Vanguard has strike, guard, burst and omen. The Dominion has them too.

Nobody in the First War has yet proved they can hold steady when it's close.

**Panel 17 — The Hook**

Rook Callahan won by eighty-four and a half points and is still called The Undrafted, because the titles in this war are not given, they are argued out of the record, one week at a time. Roman Slate lost by seventy-nine and went back to his throne and sat down.

The Dominion does not fear rebellion.

It collects rebels.

And week two is already standing at the line.

> **CAPTION — FINAL PANEL:** *Seven fronts fought. Three of them counted. The war is one week old and it has already taught the Vanguard its hardest lesson: you can win the field and barely move the line.*
