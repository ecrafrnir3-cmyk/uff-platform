# THE FIRST WAR — ISSUE #003
## "THE THRONE ANSWERS"

---

**PAGE ONE — THE THRONE, BEFORE**

There is a way a king sits after he has been beaten in public.

Roman Slate had spent a week in it. Week one had put 87.97 points on his name and taken 167.50 from a boy called Eli Thorne, and the whole Dominion court had watched the number land. 87.97. The Dynasty, routed. He had not raged. He had not explained. He sat, and the sitting was worse than rage, and the court learned to walk quietly past the doors.

**PANEL — The Compounding.** *Every advantage he takes compounds into the next.* That is his power and it is also his problem: the edifice bleeds when he falls behind. For seven days it bled.

Then the second week came, and the compounding did what compounding does.

---

**PAGE TWO — REVEILLE**

Across the field, the man they never drafted was still riding a hundred and seventy-three points.

Week one had been the best day of Rook Callahan's life and he'd have told you it was nothing. He had put 172.86 on Silas Vane's rust and walked off with 88.37 left behind him like scrap in a yard. Four feats in one week — the strike, the wall, the break, the turn of fate. Nobody had done more. One villain had done exactly as much.

"Don't say it," he told the men beside him.

"Say what?"

"That I'm due."

**PANEL — The Undrafted, wide, alone.** *He carries no gift but refusal.* It is the only power on the board with nothing in it to steal, corrode, siphon or negate.

**PAGE THREE — THE ANSWER**

*[PAGE TURN]*

It was not close for a moment.

**ROMAN SLATE 170.18 — ROOK CALLAHAN 105.28**

Roman came back onto the field with everything he had been missing. The **Explosion** — a strike that split the line open. The **Stonewall** — a wall where the Vanguard had been promised a gap. The **Breakaway** — ground taken faster than it could be given. And the **Twist of Fate**, the omen itself, the turn nobody calls for and everyone feels.

All four stat feats — everything a box score can hand a man in one afternoon, by a man who one week earlier had been counted out. There is a fifth, and nobody in this war has yet won close enough to prove their nerve.

Rook got his hands on two of them — a wall of his own, an omen of his own — and it was not near enough. Sixty-four points and ninety hundredths of a point separated the throne from the man with nothing to prove it with.

**PANEL — the close.** Roman does not gloat. That is the part that lands. He simply stands where Rook was standing, as though the ground had always been his and the week before had been a clerical error.

"Rebellion?" he says. "I don't fear it. I collect it."

Rook does not go down. He cannot; it is not in him. He is simply *moved*, the way weather moves a man, and he stands sixty-four points away and refuses to believe it while it is happening to him.

*Lost the game 105.28–170.18 · Lost the Battle — the throne never trailed.*

---

**PAGE FOUR — THE OATHKEEPER, UNBROKEN**

While the throne was entrenching itself, one of the three the war has not yet touched — the Oathkeeper, the Vampire, the Shepherd — was making it look simple.

**ELI THORNE 132.16 — SAINT VEGA 69.31**

Sixty-two points and eighty-five hundredths.

Vega came gilded, as he always comes. He had beaten Juno Reyes in week one by nineteen and change and had spent the week between telling the court it proved something. He put up sixty-nine against the Oathkeeper. He recorded nothing — not a strike, not a wall, not one turn of fate. The hoard bought him nothing, because the hoard has never bought anything. It only ever *held* things.

Eli took the **Breakaway** and the **Twist of Fate** and went about his business.

**PANEL — Eli, speaking his vow aloud, because he has to.** *Every vow he speaks aloud becomes binding.* He says nine words and not one of them is a boast.

"I said I'd hold. Hold is all it means."

Two weeks. Two wins. Both of them routs. Whatever else is true of this war, he has not yet been made to feel it.

*Won the game 132.16–69.31 · Won the Battle — the vow held.*

**PAGE FIVE — THE TIGHTEST FIGHT OF THE WAR**

*[PAGE TURN]*

Here is where the issue slows down.

Countess Mave has never had a great season of her own. She does not need one. She takes them.

Marcus Kell was built specifically not to be taken. *He soaks punishment without breaking and grows steadier the longer a battle drags.* He is the man you cannot finish, because force spends itself against him while time stays on his side. He came into week two off a loss, and he had the best week of his life: the **Explosion**, the **Breakaway**, the **Twist of Fate**. Three feats. A hundred and sixteen points and twenty-nine hundredths — nineteen more than he'd managed the week before.

It was not enough by fourteen points and thirty-three hundredths.

**COUNTESS MAVE 130.62 — MARCUS KELL 116.29**

**PANEL — the long engagement.** *A long engagement bleeds them into her.* She did not overpower him. Nobody overpowers Marcus Kell. She simply stayed, and took, and stayed, and took, and at the end of it she was carrying fourteen points that had started the afternoon in his hands.

This is the closest any cross-faction fight has come in the history of The First War. Six fights, and four of them were routs. Only two stayed close — Saint Vega over Juno Reyes by 19.29, and this. This one was a knife-edge, and she was still standing on the right side of it.

Marcus, after, on one knee, not beaten — *counting*:

"Fourteen. I'll remember fourteen."

*Lost the game 116.29–130.62 · Lost the Battle — but the Long Reckoning is a ledger, and ledgers come due.*

**PAGE SIX — THE COURT, AND THE RANKS**

Four fights that week settled nothing about the war and everything about the standing of the people in it.

**In the Dominion's court:** Ezra Cain took the Rust King down 134.80–98.50 — the Fallen Warden with a strike, a wall and a break in the same afternoon, against a man whose whole craft is patience. Silas Vane managed one **Explosion** and watched the rest corrode out from under him. The court noticed. The court always notices when one deserter beats another.

*Won the game · no front moved.*

And Vesper Kane beat Dr. Orin Pyre 118.92–104.51 — fourteen points and forty-one hundredths, the tightest thing in the Dominion's own house. The Negation, the answer key to every gift on the board, lost to a single **Twist of Fate** and a woman who has never needed a gift of her own.

*Won the game · no front moved.*

**In the Vanguard's ranks:** Juno Reyes took Titus Vale 110.32–103.00. Seven points and thirty-two hundredths — the narrowest margin anywhere in two weeks of war. The Skybreaker stole one tempo, one **Twist of Fate**, and was gone before the iron could set. Titus held a **Stonewall** and it was not the thing that mattered.

*Won the game · no front moved.*

And Brother Amos beat Cassia Dawn 126.78–102.16.

*Won the game · no front moved.*

**PANEL — the First Light, beaten by her own.** The Shepherd took a wall and an omen; the founder took a strike and an omen and the worse end of twenty-four points. *Fewer torches. Same fire.* There are fewer of them than there were, and one of them just dimmed, and the man who did it did it kindly, which is somehow harder.

None of it moved the line. That is what a duel is.

**PAGE SEVEN — THE LEDGER**

*[PAGE TURN]*

Three fights crossed the line in week two.

Roman Slate took one. Countess Mave took one. Eli Thorne took one.

**Dominion 2 — Vanguard 1.**

Put it beside week one, where Rook took Silas, and Eli took Roman, and Saint Vega took Juno:

**Vanguard 2 — Dominion 1.**

And then put them together, and look at what two weeks of maximum violence have produced.

**SIX FIGHTS. THREE EACH. THE LINE HAS NOT MOVED AN INCH.**

---

**PAGE EIGHT — THE DEADLOCK**

**PANEL — a wide, empty field at dusk. No bodies. Nobody dies here.**

Both orders came into this expecting it to be short.

The Dominion expected the reign to hold because reigns hold; that is the entire argument. The Vanguard expected the reign to crack because it stood in front of a boy who had never lost and a man who could not be convinced he had. Everyone was half right, which is the same as everyone being wrong.

Two of the worst weeks these fourteen have ever given each other. A 173-point week. An 88-point week. A throne routed and answered one week apart. Three who have not been beaten once and a man who has been beaten by sixty-four. The tightest margin in the war, fourteen points and thirty-three hundredths, and the tightest margin anywhere, seven and thirty-two.

And the front is exactly where it was the morning the war started.

**PANEL — Roman Slate, alone, back on the throne.** He wanted this. He is not sure he wanted it *this* much. *He does not fear rebellion. He collects rebels.* He has three now and needs a fourth.

**PANEL — Rook Callahan, alone, still standing, sixty-four points short.** He is not thinking about the sixty-four. He is thinking about next Sunday, which has not been played, which is the only kind of thing he has ever had.

**PANEL — the line itself. Unmoved.**

> Two weeks of maximum violence and the line has not moved an inch.
>
> The war did not begin this year and it will not end this year.
>
> But somebody is going to move it.
