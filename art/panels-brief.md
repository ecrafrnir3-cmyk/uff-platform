# The First War — COMIC PANEL Art Brief

Companion to `higgsfield-brief.md`, which is for the twenty CHARACTER PORTRAITS and must not be used for panels.

## How to use

    node scripts/generate-portraits.mjs --brief art/panels-brief.md --aspect 16:9 \
      --out public/art/panels --only 002-p01-splash --variants 2 \
      --ref public/art/characters/roman-slate.png --ref public/art/characters/cassia-dawn.png --yes

Each `##` heading below is followed by ONE self-contained prompt paragraph on a single line; the generator takes it **verbatim**, so edit the paragraph, never the script. `--ref` applies to every subject in a run, so **run one panel per invocation** with only that panel's own reference portraits.

## Why this file exists separately

The portrait brief's Framing Spec demands a 1:1 square, a tight chest-up head-and-shoulders crop and a "deep near-black atmospheric void background — no environment", and its Shared Negative Prompt forbids "not full body, no wide shot, no multiple people, no crowd". That is the exact opposite of a comic panel. Reusing it would fight every frame.

**Carried over verbatim, because it is what makes the panels read as one artist:** the Global Style Bible and the Faction Grading. **Replaced:** the framing spec and the negative prompt.

**Art only.** Captions, dialogue and the `**PAGE**` / `**Panel**` headings are rendered as HTML text by `src/components/story/ComicBody.tsx` and laid over the art. Image models mangle lettering, so nothing in frame may contain words — which also keeps the script editable after the art is paid for.

## Panel Framing Spec (fixed for all panels)
16:9 landscape comic panel, full-bleed single image with NO panel border, NO gutter and NO frame line drawn inside the picture. Cinematic staging with real depth: foreground, midground and a receding background. Figures may be full-body, mid-shot or distant as the beat requires. Leave the upper third relatively quiet and uncluttered — captions are laid over the art afterwards as text and must not be drawn.

## Shared Negative Prompt
> no text, no letters, no words, no captions, no speech balloons, no word balloons, no sound effects, no comic lettering, no title treatment, no watermark, no signature, no logo, no panel border, no panel frame, no gutters, no page layout, no multi-panel grid; no team emblems, no sports uniforms, no jerseys, no football, no stadium; no cartoon, no anime, no cel-shading, no chibi, no cute, no campy superhero spandex, no glossy plastic 3D render, no CGI sheen; no modern signifiers, no eyeglasses, no wristwatches, no zippers, no firearms; no glowing eyes, no magical energy, no floating objects, no motion streaks, no lens flare spam; no real celebrity likeness, no recognizable public figure; no extra fingers, no deformed hands, no malformed face, no asymmetric eyes, no extra limbs, no fused figures, no duplicated character in one frame; no oversaturated rainbow colours, no third accent hue, no teal, no cyan, no green, no purple, no magenta, no neon glow overload; no low detail, no blurry, no washed out, no flat lighting, no smiling stock-photo expression.

# Issue 002 — First Blood

## 1. 002 p01 splash — "Before the Line"
Grounded-mythic comic panel for a prestige graphic novel, in the vein of modern Image/Vertigo comics — painterly digital illustration with confident inked linework, not glossy 3D render and not Saturday-morning cartoon. Weathered, real human archetypes rendered with tactile texture: worn leather, hammered iron, frayed cloth, scarred skin, grime. Dramatic single-key cinematic lighting with deep shadow modeling and controlled rim light; a muted, desaturated base palette so the faction accent colours read as the only saturated notes in frame. Restrained, moody, mythic gravitas — quiet intensity over spectacle, no capes-and-tights superheroics, no generic Marvel/DC costuming. One pre-industrial mythic world of iron, leather, wool, brocade and stone, with no modern signifiers. Consistent medium-heavy detail level and one unified brush language so every panel reads as one artist, one issue. 16:9 landscape comic panel, full-bleed single image with no panel border, no gutter and no frame line drawn inside the picture, cinematic staging with real depth across foreground, midground and a receding background, and the upper third kept relatively quiet and uncluttered because captions are laid over the art afterwards. Subject — the war has no geography, only THE LINE: a wide establishing shot across an empty contested field at cold dawn, no city, no river, no banner of any named country, just bare churned ground and a gap of open air down the centre of frame that nobody has crossed yet. Camera low and wide. On the right, in the midground, the Dominion ranks stand in loose disciplined rows, and set back behind his own lines on a low dais is a heavy iron throne with ROMAN SLATE seated on it, relaxed, one forearm along the armrest, a man who has won so often that the winning has gone quiet in him — small in frame but unmistakably the still point the whole right side is arranged around. On the left, in the midground, the Vanguard line, and nearest camera CASSIA DAWN stands counting a row of guttering iron torch-staves driven into the earth beside her, several sockets standing empty and cold, her face in three-quarter profile lit by her own torchline. Fourteen figures in total, reading as a line and not a crowd, none of them looking at the camera. The left half of the frame carries the Vanguard grading: the cool cinematic dawn light IS the key, a relatively strong clean key with genuine fill so shadows stay open and the mood lifts, a thin cold pale-gold rim on the right edges of figures, electric blue (#0057FF) dominant in cloak and cloth and shadow-fill, warm gold (#FFD700) secondary restricted to metal edges and clasps and trim so it glints, desaturated steel-grey base, faint cold dawn-blue gradient in the air, exactly two accent hues with no teal or cyan or green, weathered but resolute and never shiny. The right half carries the Dominion grading: the same key direction but dim and hard with almost no fill, deep crushed shadow sides, a low sickly crimson under-kick from below the jaw, a thin blood-red rim on the right edges of figures, blood red (#CC0000) dominant, tarnished blackened gilt secondary confined to corroded metal and crowns and filigree as decayed wealth, deep matte black and oxidized brown base, a denser dirtier atmosphere with a low oxblood-brown gradient and sooty haze along the bottom, oily rust and grime in every texture, two accent hues with no purple or magenta and orange only as literal rust, restrained and sinister, gilded decay and never garish. The two gradings meet along the empty centre gap, dawn-blue bleeding into oxblood haze, and that meeting line IS the composition — the difference between the two sides must read in value and atmosphere, not hue alone. No text anywhere in the image.
